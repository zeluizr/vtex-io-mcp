Inclusive language isn't just about avoiding certain words; it's about adopting a thoughtful approach to writing that respects diversity, avoids assumptions and stereotypes, and fosters understanding by acknowledging people's identities and experiences.

## Writing principles

- **When talking to people**, be respectful of all people. Use language that considers their perspectives and experiences.
- **When talking about people**, be descriptive only when required by the context.
- **When talking about things**, use clear and accurate information, avoiding metaphors and idioms that could obscure meaning.

Avoid using terms that may carry unconscious racial bias or those associated with military actions, politics, or sensitive historical events.

| Do | Don't |
| ---- | ---- |
| Protect your data against potential threats. | Fight against cyberattacks. |
| We respond to cybersecurity risks to ensure your system's safety. | Defend your systems from the enemy. |

## Gender-neutral language

Use the singular **they** as a generic third-person singular pronoun to refer to a person whose gender is unknown or irrelevant to the context. Alternatively, **you** is also a neutral option.

Don't use *he, him, his, she, her*, or *hers* in generic references.

| Do | Don't |
| ---- | ---- |
| If a customer has questions about their order, **they** can contact our support team. | If a customer has questions about their order, **he/she** can contact our support team. |

When referring to example user profiles, use names that reflect a broad range of identities (example: Maria, Jamal) instead of only common Western names.

## Describing people

When describing a group of people, use language that reflects how they describe themselves.

If you're unsure, use person-first language, which focuses on the person before their traits. For example, say "people with disabilities" instead of "disabled people."

**To create inclusive content:**

- Focus on individuals, not their conditions or limitations.
- Use straightforward language, and steer clear of metaphors, euphemisms, or idiomatic expressions.
- Avoid words that are misinterpreted as microaggressions.
- Refrain from using terms related to disabilities to describe objects, features, or experiences.

Where appropriate:

| Do | Don't |
| ---- | ---- |
| Turn off | Disable |
| Inactive | Disabled |
| Deactivate | |

## Race

- Treat perspectives that differ from your own with curiosity and respect.
- Use neutral language and avoid associating terms like "black" or "dark" with negative connotations or "white" or "light" with positive ones.
- Be mindful of metaphors and idioms, as some may carry unintended historical or cultural biases.

## Regions

Be mindful when referring to different parts of the world. If you mention cities, countries, or regions in examples, ensure they are not politically disputed.

| Do | Don't |
| ---- | ---- |
| This feature can be used by merchants in countries like Romania, USA, and Brazil. | This feature can be used by merchants in Kashmir, Taiwan, and Crimea. |

## Terms to avoid

- **Handicap, handicapped:** Don't use to refer to someone's disability. Be specific (example: "Person who requires a keyboard-accessible checkout").
- **Blind:** Only use to reference actual blindness, not as a metaphor.
- **Insane, crazy:** Avoid using to describe something as extreme.
- **Deaf:** Only use to refer to actual deafness. Use lowercase unless referring to the Deaf community as a cultural group.
